# The TRM LaTeX ingest — design record

How the Technical Reference Manual corpus is produced, and why it is produced
that way. **Read this before touching `latex_parser.py`**: several decisions
here look like omissions until you know what they are avoiding.

The pipeline is built and the corpus is in the store. This is a record of the
design and its verification, not a plan of work.

- `latex_parser.py` resolves the include graph, evaluates tag conditionals,
  recovers **13,597 of 13,597 registers** with zero unresolved includes, and
  emits `chunking.RawChunk` through the shared
  `build_chunks`/`merge_undersized_chunks` — the same path the ESP-IDF corpus
  uses. Entry points are `resolve_document(path)` and `chunk_document(path)`;
  `chunk_latex(text, file_path)` still works.
- Registers are `Block(atomic=True)` and verified never split: 13,597 intact,
  0 split, including 28 that exceed the word cap on their own.
- `ingest_trm.py` produces **10,515 chunks across all ten manuals**, 0 thin
  files, 90% median word capture. ESP32-P4 splits into 2,394 chunks common to
  both revisions, 162 mainline-only and 142 v1.3-only, and those figures are
  confirmed present in the store.
- Porting onto the shared chunker left the ESP-IDF pipeline byte-identical
  (4,515 chunks for esp32p4, SHA-1
  `cb3e97a2b9f72a12c572aeb3bd202c802d019baf`).

Open items are listed at the end.

## Source of truth

Espressif publishes TRM LaTeX sources at
<https://github.com/espressif/esp-technical-reference-manual-latex>. This is the
input — **not** the compiled PDFs. A previous approach downloaded PDFs; both
that script and its output directory have been deleted.

Path resolution cascades, mirroring how `build_idf_docs.sh` handles `IDF_PATH`:

1. `$TRM_PATH` if set
2. `./trm_latex` — a gitignored local symlink
3. `~/git/esp-technical-reference-manual-latex`

The symlink is convenience only; absolute-path symlinks do not travel between
machines, so `TRM_PATH` is the portable mechanism.

Verified against HEAD `87b1c88` (2026-07-23): 1,550 English `.tex` files. The
ten chip directories (`ESP32/`, `ESP32-S2/`, `ESP32-S3/`, `ESP32-C3/`,
`ESP32-C5/`, `ESP32-C6/`, `ESP32-C61/`, `ESP32-H2/`, `ESP32-P4/`, `ESP8684/`)
match every non-null `trm_folder` in `chips.yaml` exactly, including the
`ESP8684` codename for esp32c2. `chip_vocab.chip_for_trm_folder()` does that
mapping during ingest.

## Why parsing the source is right here

`CLAUDE.md` states the ESP-IDF lesson emphatically: never parse `.rst`, always
consume the build. That lesson does **not** transfer to the TRM, and it is worth
being explicit about why, so nobody "fixes" this later by trying to run LaTeX.

ESP-IDF needed a build because its content dependencies are *deep*: constants
extracted from `soc_caps` headers, Kconfig values, `only::` branches selected
per target, and an entire API reference generated by doxygen from C sources.
None of that is recoverable from the `.rst` text.

The TRM's build-time dependencies are *shallow*:

- **File inclusion** — `\subfile`, `\input` and `\subfileinclude`, mechanically
  resolvable.
- **A few `\def`'d values** — e.g. `\chipname`, defined in
  `00-shared/config/preamble-trm-repo.sty`, and `\modulefiles` (a path prefix).
- **No compiler.** Nothing is extracted from another language's toolchain.

**`\ifglobal` does not gate content.** Every chapter file has exactly one
`\begin{document}`, always unconditional and after the single `\fi`; the
conditional's branches contain only `\documentclass`, `\usepackage`,
`\myexternaldocument` and comments. Skipping the preamble is safe, and this
holds repo-wide.

**`\ifglobal` is not the only conditional, and `\iftagged` *does* gate
content.** Auditing `\ifglobal` alone gives a clean result and the false
impression that the corpus has no content branching — check both before
concluding either way. See [Tag conditionals](#tag-conditionals-iftagged).

Running the real toolchain would also be a step backwards: the repo builds with
TeX Live, `minted`, Ghostscript and proprietary fonts, and produces **PDFs** —
less structure than the LaTeX source, not more.

## Chunking: descend the tree, don't assemble and split

The natural document tree is *already* at chunk granularity. Measured across all
ten chips:

| Unit | n | p50 | p90 | max | ≤400 words |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `register` environment | 11,531\* | 36 w | 146 w | 497 w | ~100% |
| Heading unit (chapter/section/subsection) | 9,231 | 96 w | 369 w | 2,560 w | 91.5% |
| Leaf `.tex` file (register/table subfiles) | 1,153 | 411 w | 2,181 w | 39,303 w | ~50% |
| Chapter `.tex` file | 390 | 2,980 w | 7,513 w | 17,181 w | ~0% |

\* Size distribution measured over the `__EN.tex` subset only. The true
population reachable via the include graph is ~13,700 — see
[filename filtering](#never-filter-includes-by-filename). The extra registers
are ordinary ones on ESP32/ESP32-S2, so the shape of the distribution holds;
only the count is understated.

Two conclusions follow.

**Do not chunk at file granularity.** Chapter files are 7–43× the 400-word cap.
Leaf files look promising at the median but have a long tail, because a single
register file holds many registers — p50 **16** registers per file, max 247.
File boundaries are an artifact of how authors organise source, not of meaning.

**Build an accurate tree and descend it.** With headings and register
environments as nodes, 91.5% of heading units and essentially 100% of registers
already fit under the cap. `chunking.build_chunks` is written this way — it
keeps a subtree whole while it fits and only splits when oversized. So there was
no new chunking algorithm to write; splitting is the fallback for the ~8.5%
tail, and it stays that way. The work is producing a faithful tree, not devising
a split strategy.

### Registers are atomic

A register is an indivisible record — name, address, bitfields, per-field
descriptions. Two consequences:

- **Never split one.** `_split_oversized_block`'s line-grouping heuristic would
  cut a large register mid-bitfield, stranding fields from the register name
  that gives them meaning. `chunking.Block.atomic` exists for this and
  `_split_paragraphs_with_refs` honours it. The ESP-IDF side benefits too — API
  `desc` blocks have identical semantics.

  This is **not** a theoretical safeguard. **28 registers exceed 400 words on
  their own** — largest 518 — and reach the splitter directly. Confirmed by a
  control: `UART_CONF0_REG` (448 words, `ESP32/19-UART__EN.tex`) survives whole
  with the flag and is cut without it. Do not remove `atomic` as unnecessary
  complexity.
- **Merging adjacent registers is fine.** At p50 36 words they fall below
  `MIN_WORDS_PER_CHUNK`, so `merge_undersized_chunks` groups them. That is
  desirable: consecutive registers belong to one peripheral and share context.
  Merge yes, split no.

## The parser, and the traps it exists to avoid

The pre-pass runs on raw text before pylatexenc, in this order: mask comments →
strip preamble → resolve `\ifglobal`/`\iffalse` → select tags → lift body
definitions → splice includes recursively. Misses and cycles land in
`ResolvedDocument.missing` rather than being swallowed.

### Inclusion resolution

Register content lives **exclusively** in included files; a chapter file on its
own is prose and almost no registers. Three include macros matter —
`\subfile`, `\input` and `\subfileinclude` (878 occurrences; missing the last
one loses whole chapters). Resolution is recursive with a cycle guard, after
substituting `\def`'d path macros.

**Strip LaTeX comments first.** Chapter headers contain literal commented-out
examples like `% '\input{\modulefiles/tables/---.tex}'`, which otherwise produce
900+ phantom unresolved includes and render the missing-include report useless.

### Document roots are the numbered chapter files only

The 379 numbered `NN-NAME__EN.tex` chapter files are the roots. Per-chip
aggregators exist — `ESP32-C3/ESP32-C3-main__EN.tex`,
`ESP32-P4/ESP32-P4-chip-revision-v1.3__EN.tex` and similar — and
`\subfileinclude` every chapter. Treating every top-level `*__EN.tex` as a
document ingests each manual two or three times over.

### Never filter includes by filename

Filter to English at the **root** level only. Included subfiles do not all
follow the `__EN`/`__CN` convention: ESP32, ESP32-S2 and ESP8684 use lowercase
`_en.tex`/`_cn.tex` (e.g. `ESP32/24-ETHERNETMAC/ethernet_reg_en.tex`), holding
**1,476 registers** that a `__EN.tex` filter drops silently. Follow the include
graph and take whatever path the include names.

This is the single most useful alarm in the census: a repo-wide count near
**11,539** means exactly this filter has been reintroduced.

### Tag conditionals (`\iftagged`)

`\iftagged{<TAG>}{<true>}{<false>}` (etoolbox) selects between alternative
content at build time. Verified in English files: **169 occurrences across 48
files** — ESP32-P4 127, ESP32-H2 21, ESP32-C5 18, every other chip 0 — and
**9 of them wrap a `\begin{register}`**. From
`ESP32-P4/56-LEDPWM/56-LEDPWM-reg__EN.tex:1`:

```latex
\iftagged{ESP32-C61}{\begin{register}{H}{LEDC\_CH\regindex{n}\_CONF0\_REG (\regindex{n}: 0-5)}{...}}%
                    {\begin{register}{H}{LEDC\_CH\regindex{n}\_CONF0\_REG (\regindex{n}: 0-7)}{...}}
```

Two different register definitions — channels 0-5 versus 0-7 — one chosen by
tag. This is a real analogue of the ESP-IDF `only::` failure mode: flattening
both branches yields duplicated, mutually contradictory register definitions in
one chunk. **`\iftagged` must be evaluated, not flattened.** The tag may name a
different chip than the containing directory (the example lives under
`ESP32-P4/` but tags on `ESP32-C61`), so selection semantics come from the
sources rather than from assuming tag == containing chip.

It also breaks the source syntactically: each such construct has two
`\begin{register}` but one `\end{register}`, since only one branch survives
compilation. Repo-wide that is 11,538 `\begin{register}` against 11,531
`\end{register}`; `56-LEDPWM-reg__EN.tex` alone has 27 versus 20. A parser that
assumes balanced environments may error, mis-nest, or silently swallow the rest
of the file — the last being the dangerous outcome.

Tags are declared by `\usetag{\chipseries}` in
`00-shared/config/preamble-trm-repo.sty`, so a chip's own name is always active,
plus `\usetag{ESP32-P4-latest}` in `ESP32-P4-main__EN.tex`. Two-argument
`\tagged`/`\untagged` forms exist as well as `\iftagged`.

Verified outcome: `ESP32-P4/56-LEDPWM__EN.tex` yields 20 registers from 27
`\begin{register}`, correctly selecting the `0-7` branch rather than
ESP32-C61's `0-5`.

### Three bugs the design did not anticipate

All invisible in word counts, all of the silent-corruption class this project
keeps meeting:

1. **`&` was being dropped**, so `_render_table`'s `split("&")` never split and
   *every table row rendered as one run-on cell*. Pre-existing.
2. **A `\newcommand` body opening an environment closed at the use site.** The
   GPIO chapters define a macro containing `\begin{small}\begin{longtable}…`
   with no `\end`; pylatexenc scanned for the missing `\end{small}` and
   swallowed **128,896 characters — 221 registers vanished with no error.**
3. **`lstlisting` was shredded**, because pylatexenc's verbatim parser hardcodes
   `\end{verbatim}`, so listing bodies were parsed as LaTeX.

### Fixed: line-wrapped register headers

`_render_register` preserved a source line-wrap inside an argument, so a
register whose name is written across two lines emitted its address on the
block's second line, where every line-wise consumer read the header as
address-less:

```latex
\begin{register}{H}{MCPWM\_TIMER\regindex{n}\_SYNC\_REG⏎(\regindex{n}: 0-2)}⏎{0x000C+0x10*\regindex{n}}
```

`_render_one_line` now collapses newlines for header fields (register name,
address, and each `\regfield` name/width/bitpos/reset). A full text diff of all
13,597 rendered registers showed exactly **6 changed**, all intended: the 2
MCPWM headers rejoined, and 4 RISC-V `pmpXcfg` blocks that genuinely have an
empty address argument upstream now rendering `Register X` rather than a
dangling `Register X at address `. Parameterised registers went from 1,154/1,156
to **1,156/1,156** rendering with name and address.

## Macro coverage

`latex_coverage_check.py <root>` reports every macro and environment used in a
tree that the parser does not explicitly handle, most frequent first. The
directory to scan is a **required positional argument**, so it can be pointed at
one chip or at the whole repo:

```bash
uv run latex_coverage_check.py "$TRM_PATH"              # or: just latex-coverage
uv run latex_coverage_check.py "$TRM_PATH/ESP32-C3"     # one chip
uv run latex_coverage_check.py "$TRM_PATH" --top-n 60   # default is 40
```

Coverage was the main body of work. Before it was done, ESP32-C3 alone reported
100 unknown macros and 14 unknown environments, and the whole repo roughly 240
and 28 — the parser had clearly been written against a small sample. Measured
against the current parser: **ESP32-C3 (126 files) reports 33 unknown macros and
1 unknown environment; the full repo (1,550 files) reports 149 and 4.** What
remains is dominated by things that are correctly ignorable — `\modulefiles`
(already resolved by the include pre-pass), `\def`, and maths operators such as
`\times`, `\in` and `\cdots`.

The grouping that drove the work, and which is the right way to read a fresh
report:

**Ignorable (drop or pass through).** `\centering`, `\bfseries`, `\it`,
`\textcolor`, `\rotatebox`, `\fbox`, `\scriptsize`, `\includegraphics` (alt text
unavailable), and `bytefield` — a diagram package, not a register
representation.

**Preamble scaffolding (skipped wholesale before `\begin{document}`).**
`\documentclass`, `\usepackage`, `\newif`/`\globaltrue`/`\ifglobal`,
`\myexternaldocument`, `\selectlanguage`, `\setcounter`, `\let`, `\relax`,
`\def`, `\tableofcontents`.

**Must handle — content is lost otherwise.** `\subfile`/`\input`/
`\subfileinclude`/`\modulefiles`; `\verb` (inline verbatim, needs its own lexing
because the delimiter is the next character); `\endhead`/`\endfirsthead`/
`\endfoot`/`\endlastfoot` (`longtable` repeated-header machinery, which
otherwise duplicates header rows into body content); `\tnote` with
`threeparttable`/`ThreePartTable`/`tablenotes` (a second table family beyond
`longtable`/`tabular`); `math`/`equation`/`gather*` (TRM prose contains real
inline maths); `\caption{...}`; `\chipname` and other `\def`'d values — the
TRM's `{IDF_TARGET_*}` analogue, without which the literal macro name lands in
the embedding; and `\iftagged`.

Treat anything appearing more than ~50 times in a fresh report as required.

## Figures and diagrams

**`bytefield` is not a second register representation.** 31 occurrences in 6
files, confined to ESP32 (21), ESP32-P4 (4), ESP32-S2 (2) and ESP32-S3 (2);
zero in C3, C5, C6, C61, H2 and ESP8684. Every occurrence sits inside a
`\begin{figure}`, and the usage is CPU *instruction encoding* (ULP coprocessor
opcode layouts) or DMA *descriptor* layouts — not memory-mapped peripheral
registers. Those same chapters carry their real registers in `\subfile`d
`-reg__EN.tex` files using the ordinary `register` environment (e.g. 59 in
`ESP32/24-ETHERNETMAC/ethernet_reg_en.tex`). Ignoring `bytefield` loses no
register content.

**Figures lose no register data, but captions are worth extracting.** No case
was found where a register bit layout exists *only* as a figure: every
register-ish figure duplicates a `register` environment or a table documented in
the same chapter. The figure population is overwhelmingly block/architecture and
timing diagrams. 1,703 of 1,754 figure environments (97%) carry a descriptive
caption — "UART Architecture Overview", "GDMA Engine Architecture", "TDM PCM
Standard Timing Diagram" — so captions are extracted and the images themselves
are accepted as unrecoverable.

## Silicon revisions: ingest both P4 variants and deduplicate

**Ingest every variant, dedup by `(file_path, text)`, and record applicability
in a `revisions` list column** — the same mechanism `chips` already uses, one
axis over. Content common to both variants stores once listing both; content
that differs stays separate and correctly narrower.

This is not optional polish. The register sets diverge **in both directions**:

| | registers |
|---|---|
| Common to both | 2,873 |
| Only in v1.3 | **48** |
| Only in mainline | **60** |

Picking a single variant silently loses real registers either way — v1.3's
`AHB_DMA_ARB_TIMEOUT_RX_REG`/`TX_REG` became one `AHB_DMA_ARB_TIMEOUT_REG` in
mainline. Someone debugging v1.3 silicon against mainline-only docs would be
looking up a register their part does not have. Distinct register-env counts
(3,977 vs 3,990) also show same-named registers whose bitfields changed, which
only text-level dedup catches — name comparison would miss them.

The alternative of two pseudo-chips (`esp32p4`, `esp32p4-v1.3`) was rejected;
see
[architecture.md](architecture.md#project-history--what-was-tried-and-deleted).

### Labels are `v1.3` and `mainline`, never "latest"

The source names them itself, in
`ESP32-P4/00-chip-spec-content/chip-spec-settings.sty`:

```latex
\newcommand\chiprevisionEN{%
    \untagged{ESP32-P4-latest}{Chip Revision v1.3}%
}%
```

Untagged builds are titled "Chip Revision v1.3"; the tagged build carries **no
revision qualifier at all** — it is the mainline manual. `ESP32-P4-latest` is
the tag's internal name and was never a published label, so storing "latest"
would invent a version string Espressif does not use *and* rot the moment new
silicon ships. Store `v1.3` and `mainline`, and let `source_commit` pin down
what `mainline` meant at ingest time.

### Deriving the variant

`ingest_trm.py` drives this from the aggregator documents' own `\usetag`
declarations rather than hand-passed tags, and warns when the source disagrees
with `chips.yaml` — that file drives the MCP revision filter, so drift would
mislabel results.

- `ESP32-P4-main__EN.tex` → `\usetag{ESP32-P4-latest}` → `revisions=["mainline"]`
- `ESP32-P4-chip-revision-v1.3__EN.tex` → no tag → `revisions=["v1.3"]`

Chapters are still ingested individually (the 379 numbered files), but the tag
context comes from the aggregator, so nobody has to remember to pass
`extra_tags`. Chips with a single manual get `revisions=["mainline"]` so
`array_contains` works uniformly — do not leave it empty for them, or the
empty-list trap that `chips` was redesigned to remove reappears here.

The other tags (`ESP32-C61`, `ESP32-C5`, `ESP32-S31`, `ESP32-H21`, `ESP32-H4`)
are a *different* use: chip selection inside shared register files, already
handled by `\usetag{\chipseries}`. Only `ESP32-P4-latest` is a revision axis.

## Verification

Gating everything above, per
[verify output, don't trust counts](architecture.md#verify-output-dont-trust-counts).
Commands and expected readings are in
[building-the-corpus.md](building-the-corpus.md#verify-before-you-trust-it);
this is what each one is *for*.

- **Capture rate per file** — extracted words against the source's own text
  content. This is the method that caught the `field_list` bug on the ESP-IDF
  side, where 4,222 nodes were being silently dropped while totals looked
  healthy. `check_thin_files.py latex` covers it (`xml` is the ESP-IDF
  equivalent; they are separate subcommands because a Sphinx page is one
  self-contained file whereas a TRM chapter is a `.tex` plus everything it
  includes).
- **Coverage gate** — `latex_coverage_check.py` across all chips, with a hard
  look at anything frequent remaining unknown.
- **Register spot-checks** — assert known registers survive intact, e.g.
  `I2C_SCL_LOW_PERIOD_REG` at `0x0000` in
  `ESP32-C3/01-I2C/01-I2C-reg__EN.tex`: name, address and every bitfield in one
  chunk.
- **Register census** — `register_census.py`, counting how many source registers
  survive into chunks, **per chip**, since the failure modes are chip-specific:
  filename convention on ESP32/ESP32-S2, `\iftagged` on ESP32-P4. It exits
  non-zero past a shortfall threshold.

Two matching hazards, both of which read downstream as content loss:

- Register names nest braces (`SPI\_MEM\_C\_{n}\_REG` for register banks, ~17%
  of all registers). A non-nesting `\{[^}]*\}` capture truncates them. Match
  with brace balancing.
- Register names routinely contain spaces and parentheses —
  `Register AES_KEY_n_REG (n: 0-7) at address 0x0000+4*n`. A pattern like
  `Register (AES_[^\s]+) at address` therefore matches nothing for any
  parameterised register and reports the ~1,156 of them as absent when they
  render perfectly. **Validate a missing-content check against a register known
  to be present before concluding anything about the parser.**

### Reconciling two register counts

`register_census.py source` reports **13,707** because it greps
`\begin{register}` before tag selection; the parser emits **13,597** after it.
The 110-register difference is unselected `\iftagged` branches and is *correct*.
It is 0.8%, comfortably under the census's 2% threshold, so a faithful ingest
still passes — but don't go hunting for the gap. A count near **11,539** is the
real alarm; see [filename filtering](#never-filter-includes-by-filename).

### `trm_verify` deliberately does not import `latex_parser`

`trm_verify.py` holds the source-side helpers the TRM checkers share, and it
resolves includes itself. A checker that shared the parser's include resolution
could not detect a bug in it. **If its source-side counting needs fixing, fix it
in that module — never by importing the parser**, which would forfeit the
independence that makes the census worth running.

The census long reported a small shortfall on a *good* ingest, concentrated in
the two AES chapters and `ESP32-H2/33-USBSERIALJTAG`: registers disabled
upstream inside `\iffalse`, or tagged for a different chip, which
`latex_parser` correctly excluded while `trm_verify.expand_document` still
counted them. Teaching `expand_document` to evaluate those two constructs closes
the gap, and `just census-check` was measured clean — no missing registers —
once it did.

The durable lesson outlives the numbers: **a census can legitimately count
source the parser is right to drop, so a shortfall is not by itself evidence of
content loss.** Confirm what the named registers actually are before treating
one as a regression.

### The expected reading

`just census-check` on a correct ingest reports **10,092 of 10,092 registers, 0
missing, 100.0%**, ending with `OK: 0.0% shortfall, within the 2.0% threshold.`
Per chip: ESP32 625, ESP32-C3 752, ESP32-C5 1328, ESP32-C6 1124, ESP32-C61 667,
ESP32-H2 1027, ESP32-P4 2334, ESP32-S2 826, ESP32-S3 978, ESP8684 431 — each at
100.0%.

That is an exact figure, not a tolerance, so any shortfall at all is worth
investigating rather than waved through as noise. The 2% threshold exists to
catch a catastrophic regression loudly; it is not a budget to spend.

`just census-source` counts the source side alone and reports **13,687**
registers across 379 chapters with zero unresolved includes. Two numbers, both
correct: `source` counts every `\begin{register}` the include graph reaches after
conditionals are evaluated, while `check` compares only non-parameterised names
against the chunk output. A source count near **11,539** is the alarm — that
means includes are being filtered by `__EN` filename again.

### The checkers are themselves tested

`make_trm_fixture.py` synthesises a faithful corpus and a deliberately broken
one from the real LaTeX, and both checkers must tell them apart. See
[development.md](development.md#fixtures--proving-the-checkers-still-discriminate).

## Still open

- **Cross-references.** `\label`/`\hyperref` could populate `doc_refs`, giving
  TRM chunks the same graph affordance ESP-IDF chunks have. Deferred, not
  broken — TRM chunks currently carry no `doc_refs` at all.
- **Diagram content.** ULP instruction-format and DMA-descriptor `bytefield`
  diagrams, and block/timing diagrams generally, remain unrecoverable as
  structured data. Captions partly compensate. Accepted limitation.
- **The census's expected reading** — to be pinned here once the
  `\iffalse`/inactive-`\tagged` fix is settled; see the note above.
